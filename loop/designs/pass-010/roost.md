# roost

**Premise.** A giant porcelain hen dozes on a floor as thin as a plate, a second place hangs upside down from the underside of that same floor, and the camera rises through a missing shard from one to the other like one of her eggs.

## Ten premises, five struck

1. ~~A giant tea table; fly down the teapot's spout.~~ Porcelain means tea set: everyone's first picture.
2. ~~A bull asleep in a china shop.~~ The stock joke for "clunky" plus "porcelain".
3. ~~A porcelain theatre; fly through the stage curtain as it rises.~~ The light read literally.
4. ~~Inside a kiln, pots glowing as they fire.~~ Material to factory; also a glowing cave.
5. ~~A glaze lake that mirrors an upside-down copy of the world.~~ The law read as a reflection.
6. A bath the size of a lake, a hippo asleep in it, suds rising.
7. A hill-sized sleeping piggy bank; you are the coin and the exit is its slot.
8. A broken, gold-mended willow plate whose painted landscape you fly over; the exit is the missing shard.
9. The lap of a curled porcelain dormouse; the camera circles inside the curl and leaves through its ear.
10. A hen-shaped covered dish asleep on a two-sided floor: below, her bowl hangs as a lamp in an upside-down place; the exit is the gap when the lid, the hen, floats off.

**Chosen: 10.** It is the only one where the kit's law is the plot: the climb passes through the shared floor and the lamp you flew under turns out to be a nest with a hen on it. And its exit is prepared for fifteen seconds as a closed bowl you have seen from underneath, so the world inside it is plainly impossible; the others are one picture each.

- **The hen**: centre H = E + 11 along the exit heading = (-10, 12.5, -41.3). Nest lip radius 11, so E is on the lip. She is 26 long, 15 tall, head to the east, in profile from the south.
- **The lamp**: the underside of her nest bowl, a dome of radius 10.6 hanging 9 below the ceiling (bottom y = 3.3).
- **The missing shard**: a five-sided hole in the floor, about x -13..9, z -12.5..6.5, gilt broken edge. The camera crosses the slab inside it at t = 16.5 to 16.8 and flies on over it until z = -12 (t = 19.6).
- **The curtain**: a pleated cylinder at radius 170 running up and down from the floor plane; its hem stops 2.5 short of the floor on both sides and that gap is the light.

The approach (t = 5 to 7) passes underneath the very floor the camera flies over at t = 19.

## Opening silhouette (t = 0 to 5)

The camera is level, 12.3 below a ceiling. Upper half: a flat white ceiling. Lower half: moss dark. Between them a gold line. Hanging from the white into the dark, an upside-down skyline of crockery stacks; dead ahead a column of gold light falling through the hole, full of rising eggs; at the left edge the lamp, a glowing half-disc hanging from the ceiling like a sunset the wrong way up, which the 26 degree sweep carries to the centre by t = 2.5. It reads small because it is three flat bands and one half-disc, bright on dark, and against the orbit's indigo it is the only white thing on screen.

## Inhabitants

- **Eggs** (one instanced draw, about 360). Each has a column, a period of 14 to 30 s and a phase; `u = fract(t / period + phase)`. From u = 0 to 0.55 it rises from y = -22 and slows under the ceiling; 0.55 to 0.70 it passes through the slab, its waist pinched at the floor plane (a per-vertex scale that depends on height above the floor), so one egg is seen sinking into the ceiling below and budding from the floor above; 0.70 to 0.78 it rocks on the floor like a roly-poly toy; then it lifts off, rises 30 and shrinks to nothing. Sideways drift is two sines, the swoon is a slow tilt of up to 20 degrees, the wobble a 5 percent squash. Eggs whose column is inside the hole (flagged at mount) skip the pinch and the rest. Each is painted from its seed: cobalt bands, dots, zigzags, a few gilt, a few with one persimmon spot. Sizes 0.15 to 1.2. Pushed out of a cone ahead of the eye, as in `helix/koi.ts`.
- **The hen** breathes (the body swells 1.5 percent on a 5 s sine) and nods off (the head sinks for 5 s and jerks back in 1.25 s, four times in the 25). Her eye is a closed cobalt arc with lashes; she never looks at anything.
- **Stacks**: about 70 towers of cups, bowls, saucers, jars and a teapot, 5 to 14 pieces each; half stand on the top face and teeter, half hang from the underside at mirrored spots and swing. Bend angle is `A * sin(w t + phase + k.xz)` times height along the stack to the power 1.5, so a slow swoon travels across the field.

## The exit

The object is a hen-on-nest covered dish: the nest is the dish, the hen is the lid. The craft is all on her and on the lip: a basket-weave rim in low relief with a gilt edge; a hem of scalloped feather tips, each outlined in cobalt and edged in gold; painted feather arcs over a white glaze that pools celadon in the hollows; comb and wattle in persimmon; fine crackle that only shows close.

It is too thin twice over. Until t = 20 the opening is a 0.06 line of light under a lid. And the viewer has flown under the floor and round the only space the dish has: a bowl 9 deep, shut, glowing, that cannot hold a stair shaft.

The portal is a static upright cylinder wall of radius 10.9 inside the lip, from the lip to 6 above it, wearing `portalMaterial`; her skirt hides it until she rises. Because the portal samples in screen space, the curved wall shows a flat, true window. Lift: 0 until 20; two hops of 0.12 with a degree of tilt at 20.1 and 20.6; then `3.6 * smoothstep(20.9, 24.6, t)` with a 4 degree lean back. The camera needs no aiming: the rail brings E to centre by t = 23 and the last 4.76 units are straight at the lip. At t = 24 the hem is above the frame; the lip leaves the bottom at 24.4.

## How it is built

- **One environment function** `curtain(dir, t)`: pleats by azimuth, moss darkening away from the floor plane, the gold seam at the horizon (its gap widens from 1.5 to 4 between t = 5 and 25: the curtain is starting to go up). It draws the backdrop sphere and is sampled by every glaze shader as a Fresnel reflection, so the seam becomes a long highlight on every round thing. This is what says "glazed".
- **Light through porcelain**: the bowl is emissive (1.6 to 2.5) through a weave-thickness pattern; the ceiling takes an analytic glow `k / (1 + d^2)` from the lamp; eggs get wrap lighting and a rim from the seam. No real lights, no shadow maps.
- **One baked 1024 float map of the floor**, built at mount: R is distance to the mended cracks (thresholded with `fwidth`, so the gold stays crisp at 4K), G is contact shade under every stack and round the nest, B is the cobalt border rings centred on the hen. Both faces and the broken edge read the same map, so a crack is seen to be the same crack on both sides.
- Meshes: backdrop sphere; floor (two faces and the hole's edge wall); hen (displaced ellipsoid body, lathed tail plumes, head, comb, wings in relief, about 45k triangles); nest lip and bowl; portal wall; stacks merged into two static geometries, near (48 segments) and far (20 segments), with pivot, height and seed as vertex attributes for the sway; eggs instanced; the light column (one additive cone, faded near the eye); gilt motes.
- Glow: seam 4 to 6, gilt and cracks 2.5, bowl about 2, the fan of light on the floor before the gap (analytic, scaled by the lift). White glaze tops out at 0.9; suggested look: bloom 1.2, threshold 1.15, vignette 0.28.
- Count: about 12 draw calls and 630k triangles (stacks 390k, eggs 170k, hen and nest 57k). Everything is opaque but the column and motes, and every shader is closed-form, so the estimate is 1.4 to 1.8 ms at 4K.

## The kit in the result

- **drifting**: the eggs, the camera's own spiral among them, and at last the hen, all drift up on one slow current.
- **clunkiest**: the heaviest, most kitsch thing in the world is the one that floats, after rattling her own lid twice; the stacks teeter.
- **swooning**: she nods off and jerks back; her rise is a lean backwards; eggs tip as they go; a sway passes through the stacks.
- **two places share one floor, one upside down**: the slab has two faces, the under one hung with a hanging world, and the camera crosses it at t = 16.6.
- **porcelain and glaze**: everything is it: translucent bowl, pooled celadon, underglaze cobalt, gilt, gold-mended cracks, a biscuit underside.
- **the size of one giant sleeping animal**: the whole 25 seconds is one lap under, beside and into a single hen.
- **the last light before a curtain rises**: a seam of gold under a curtain all round the horizon, and a hairline under the hen's hem; the scene is the minute before, and the curtain that rises is her.
- **they drift up like bubbles**: the eggs, including through the floor like bubbles through a film.
- **start from the exit**: it began as a slit of light under a lid; the lid became a hen, her dish needed an underside, and the underside became the first place.

## Risks, and the first cuts

1. **The hen carries the scene.** A lumpy hen sinks it. Build her first, judge her as a weathervane silhouette at t = 20 and at thumbnail size, and keep her forms as plain as a pressed-glass dish.
2. **The under-place could read as a dim cave of stalactites, with an empty lower half.** Keep the ceiling flat, bright and gilt-cracked, the stacks unmistakably crockery, the haze pale, and the eggs dense below eye level.
3. **The crossing and the gap are tight geometry.** The hole must clear the track wherever the eye is within 1.5 of the slab (t = 15.6 to 17.3), and the lift must clear the frame by 24.4; white glaze must stay under the bloom threshold while the seam blows out.

Cut first, in order: motes and crackle; the far stacks' painted variety; the egg pinch (let eggs simply pass through the slab); the hen's nod.
