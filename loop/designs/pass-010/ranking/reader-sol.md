Ranking: C, A, B, D

C — roost
The strongest point is that the climb changes our understanding of an object: the overhead lamp becomes the sleeping hen’s nest, then the hen becomes a lid, giving the ride a sequence of discoveries rather than just changing scenery. The ceiling, hanging crockery and rising eggs make an effective opening stamp; the porcelain animal, cobalt drawing and white/moss field distinguish it from helix’s stone shaft, burrow’s flesh tunnel, dollhouse’s sunny garden and orbit’s inhabited night cylinder, although its inverted settlement brushes against orbit’s premise. The crafted feather hem and basket lip make the exit a satisfying joke, and seeing the dish’s underside earns the impossible interior, though the enormous hen makes “too small” less immediate than A’s bubble or B’s flame. Its biggest risk is execution of the underworld and hen silhouette: repeated hanging stacks could become a monotonous cave before the reveal, while the slab crossing and lid clearance need precise geometry; the mostly opaque, mount-built approach is plausible within the GPU and code budgets, but its quoted timings remain estimates.
Scores: (a) 9/10; (b) 9/10; (c) 9/10; (d) 10/10; (e) 9/10; (f) 8/10.

A — slurp
The strongest point is the crazy straw: it turns the prescribed approach–loop–approach into a recognizable, funny object connecting a sleeper to an exquisitely small bubble exit. A glowing yellow floor beneath a long-eared sleeper should read well inside the indigo moon gate, and the tea-tray premise, green/lemon lighting and hopping inhabitants separate it from all four existing worlds, although the central pot risks recalling helix’s column and the crockery curls could resemble dollhouse’s candy-like ornament. The upward pour, shaded tops and hopping bodies offer plenty to watch in silence, but “gravity points toward whatever is nearest” is less reliably communicated than the simpler impression of upside-down tea; its biggest risk is competing effects obscuring that visual story, compounded by sinister underlit faces. The bubble’s striped flower-tip, breathing film and tiny supporting stem are the best literal too-thin exit here, while bespoke glaze, transparent glass, film, articulated hops and several lighting tricks make the budget and roughly 50 kB code allowance less secure than the triangle count alone suggests.
Scores: (a) 9/10; (b) 9/10; (c) 8/10; (d) 9/10; (e) 10/10; (f) 7/10.

B — wick
The strongest point is the legged reflection carrying an unlit candle: it is an impossible, friendly joke with a clear visual comparison, and the white/black/vermilion ballroom is exceptionally distinct from helix’s masonry, burrow’s organic enclosure, dollhouse’s garden and orbit’s blue town. The turn joining the dance and the climb revealing a flower of flames could be hypnotic, while the striped taper’s melt crown and tiny flame provide a carefully crafted exit that convincingly cannot contain the sandstone shaft. Its biggest risk is that the fixed floor lies roughly sixteen units below the eye late in the climb, so the shoes and paired candles become distant precisely when the proposal needs its central joke to remain legible; even the promised black lower half of the opening stamp competes with a grazing-angle floor that turns white. Mirrored geometry avoids a costly reflection pass and fits the deterministic rules, but transparency ordering, dense flames and smoke, and keeping white-on-white depth throughout the final approach make the claimed 4K timing uncertain; it is more likely to become a beautiful mirror demonstration than C or A is to lose its personality.
Scores: (a) 8/10; (b) 10/10; (c) 6/10; (d) 7/10; (e) 9/10; (f) 7/10.

D — earshot
The strongest point is the sewn-leaf pocket: a luminous veined enclosure with house-sized stitches is a distinctive place, and the thread opening into a leaf-shaped window is an elegant, exceptionally thin exit with useful craft details in the crook and acorn. Its green/gold membranes and party-horn inhabitants differ from helix’s stone, dollhouse’s garden and orbit’s town, but the living enclosure risks burrow’s territory, the coil repeats helix’s central spring silhouette, and emitted rings lean toward the tech-demo imagery the brief rejects. Its biggest risk is semantic: in silence, moving closer while unrolling and emitting rings can read as greeting or growth rather than distance being loudness, and a mostly hushed opening with its conductor moving across the central view is less dependable at postage-stamp size than the other openings. The climb usefully changes the great leaf from roof to walkway and the turn exposes the stitches, with credible instancing and analytic animation, but hundreds of rings, thin antialiased strands and blended membranes still need fill-rate discipline, while cutting solo toots would weaken the requirement for continuous life.
Scores: (a) 7/10; (b) 8/10; (c) 5/10; (d) 8/10; (e) 9/10; (f) 8/10.

I would borrow A’s shared breathing rhythm for C: let the hen’s breath subtly drive the nest’s glow and the nearby eggs’ rise, then let that same rhythm grow into the two lid rattles and final lift. This gives the long underside passage a visible relationship between inhabitant, light and motion before the hen is revealed, without adding another prop family or an explanatory effect. Keep the breathing gentle and subordinate to the floor-crossing reveal so the borrowed idea strengthens C’s existing story.
