Ranking: C, A, B, D

C — roost

Its strongest point is that the prescribed climb becomes a revelation: an upside-down lamp becomes the underside of a sleeping hen’s dish, and the final level approach pays that discovery off by lifting the lid. Rising eggs, wobbling crockery and the hen’s sleepy double rattle make the impossible space friendly and funny without needing sound; the opening’s white ceiling, dark lower field and hanging luminous bowl should survive thumbnail reduction, although the hen herself arrives late. White glaze, cobalt brushwork and an animal-shaped exit separate it from sandstone helix, fleshy burrow, pastel garden dollhouse and indigo town orbit, though inversion shares orbit’s territory and the thin-container reveal shares dollhouse’s. The crafted feather hem and basket rim are excellent, but a hill-sized dish is less immediately “too small” than A’s bubble, and the biggest risk is viewers failing to connect the distant early bowl to the late hen; mostly opaque, mount-built geometry and analytic movement look feasible within roughly 50 kB and the frame budget, subject to measuring the dense stacks and solving the tight floor crossing and portal clearance.
Scores: (a) 9/10; (b) 8/10; (c) 8/10; (d) 10/10; (e) 8/10; (f) 8/10.

A — slurp

Its strongest point is the recognizable crazy straw, dozing long-eared creature and tiny dream bubble: there is an appealing visual story even if nobody infers the nearest-surface gravity rule. The sleeper against a yellow floor and green sky is the strongest proposed opening mascot, and the handle passage, climb around a squat teapot and return view down onto the straw make good use of all three track sections. Porcelain, vermilion striping and hoppers distinguish it from helix’s masonry and fish, burrow’s living tunnel, dollhouse’s paper garden and orbit’s night town, although toy scale could drift toward dollhouse and looping around a central object recalls helix. The petalled straw tip and breathing film make the most convincingly tiny crafted exit here, but the biggest risk is overload from competing miracles and a transparent straw that fails to read; the instanced, clock-driven build is plausible within the code allowance, while full-screen shading, film and transparency make the asserted GPU cost an estimate rather than evidence.
Scores: (a) 9/10; (b) 8/10; (c) 8/10; (d) 9/10; (e) 10/10; (f) 7/10.

B — wick

Its strongest point is a coherent comic impossibility demonstrated repeatedly: reflections have shoes and do the work while unlit candles ride above them, and the dance becoming a flower offers sustained unfolding. The milk-white, black and vermilion ballroom, wax inhabitants and flame exit are unusually distinct from all four existing worlds; architectural grandeur overlaps helix and luminous points overlap orbit, but neither dominates the premise, and it avoids both burrow’s organic enclosure and dollhouse’s pastel garden. The opening has strong value structure, yet the prescribed grazing approach makes the crucial shoes and lit/unlit mismatch difficult to see, while the climb that reveals the dance pattern also takes us away from its funniest details. The taper’s melt crown and flame window are beautifully small and crafted, and duplicated geometry avoids a reflection render pass, but the biggest risk is a polished-floor tech-demo reading rather than an impossible living space; white-on-white depth, floor overdraw and smoke need careful budgeting, though shared shaders make roughly 50 kB credible, and lowering the camera as its fallback suggests is unavailable under the fixed curve.
Scores: (a) 8/10; (b) 9/10; (c) 6/10; (d) 8/10; (e) 9/10; (f) 7/10.

D — earshot

Its strongest point is the sewn-leaf material world, especially the horizon becoming stitches and a single humming hair becoming an exit; green transmission, leaf horns and an acorn bob distinguish it from the four current palettes and populations. The climb from beneath a leaf onto its midrib uses the track sensibly, but the coil repeats helix’s central spiral structure, the enclosing membrane risks burrow-like spatial sameness, and botanical whimsy approaches dollhouse, while the bright continuous illumination clearly separates it from orbit. The biggest risk is fundamental legibility: without sound, unrolling horns, rings and radial motion can show friendly creatures approaching, but do not reliably establish that loudness changes distance, and the initially off-centre conductor may miss the narrow opening window despite its strong silhouette. The thread’s fittings are convincingly crafted and impossibly thin, yet its analytic blur could look like a generic glowing portal; mount-baked veins and instancing make the code and geometry budgets plausible, while hundreds of rings, thin-edge aliasing and layered transmission need measurement, and the fallback to growing in place weakens the spatial premise further.
Scores: (a) 7/10; (b) 8/10; (c) 5/10; (d) 8/10; (e) 9/10; (f) 7/10.

I would borrow A’s shared breathing rhythm for C: let the hen’s breath produce a subtle synchronized pulse in the bowl’s transmitted light, visible underneath early and in the illuminated nest lip after the climb. That gives viewers a repeatable visual clue that the lamp and hen belong to the same object, strengthening C’s main reveal without another prop or sound. Keep the pulse gentle enough that the two lid rattles remain distinct events and final portal coverage stays deterministic.
